Before Anything Is Generated

Clearance gates — all three must close first

This video is a synthetic performance of a real, deceased artist. These are not post-production formalities; nothing should be rendered until they are signed.

  • Estate approval — written consent for the digital likeness from the Houston estate. Post-mortem right of publicity is enforced in the relevant jurisdictions and this use is squarely within it.
  • Master recording license — sync rights for the 2009 recording, plus Akon's likeness consent as a living artist.
  • Synthetic media disclosure — a visible AI-generation card at head or tail, plus platform-level synthetic content flags on upload. Required by every major distributor. Design it in; don't bolt it on.

If any gate cannot close, this book still works as a live-action treatment with a performer, or as an abstract//typographic piece with no likeness at all.

Concept

The story

A woman alone in a high room above a raining city. She remembers. She decides. She goes back. She hesitates at the door — and the door opens before she knocks.

The revision from the previous board: the arc no longer rises unbroken. A doubt beat sits immediately before arrival. Two seconds of not-knocking is what makes the ending land.

The ending, changed

We no longer show a reunion. We end on the door opening, warm light crossing her face, and cut to black on the final vocal.

Two reasons. It is the stronger read — the title is a question the audience should close themselves. And it removes the single hardest shot to generate convincingly: a sustained two-person embrace with faces in contact.

Structure — The Chorus Is The Spine

The previous board had seven narrative beats and no choruses. A music video is a timeline before it is a story. Below, the story sits inside the song's sections.

The rule: one recurring image — she sings to camera at the rain-streaked window — returns at every chorus, and changes each time. Chorus 1 she is still. Chorus 2 she is already leaving mid-line. Chorus 3 the frame is inverted: same composition, but she is outside in the rain. That inversion is the whole video in one cut.

Timecodes below are a working template. Conform them to your actual master before generating — every shot length depends on it.

Colour Design

Four palettes, each assigned to a story state. Colour carries the narrative rather than decorating it — and the final frame is the only one where two palettes coexist.

Identity Lock Protocol

Drift across shots is the primary failure mode of this approach and the reason most AI music videos read as fake. Treat identity as an asset to be version-controlled.

  1. Build the reference set first. 8–12 stills, locked: front, 3/4 left, 3/4 right, profile, plus two lit exactly like the COLD key and two like the WARM key. Nothing is generated until this set is approved and frozen.
  2. One seed family per palette. Do not re-roll seeds across a section — drift compounds invisibly and only shows in the edit.
  3. Face pass on every shot. Re-reference against the locked set after generation, before lip sync.
  4. Reject, don't fix. If a clip's identity is off, regenerate. Patching a drifted face in post costs more than a re-roll and never fully works.
  5. Continuity ledger. Track per shot: earring, ring, hair part, gown strap position. These drift silently and break the cut.

Pipeline — Order Matters

  1. Lock references — identity set approved and frozen. Nothing before this.
  2. Generate at base resolution. Do not upscale yet.
  3. Conform to the master. Cut the whole video at base res against the real audio. Find the edit before you spend a single upscale credit.
  4. Lip sync — at base res, on locked picture only. Syncing shots that later get trimmed is wasted money.
  5. Upscale to 4K — only the shots that survived the edit.
  6. Retime and motion. Conform everything to 24fps. Generated clips arrive at mixed cadences; fix with optical flow, not frame duplication.
  7. Grade in Resolve — palette assignment per the colour design, then halation, gate weave and 35mm grain last.
  8. Disclosure card and delivery encodes.

The expensive mistake: upscaling before the edit is locked. Sync before upscale, edit before sync.

QC — Per-Shot Rejection Checklist

Identity & anatomy

  • Face matches locked reference under this shot's key light
  • Hands: five fingers, correct scale, no morph across the clip
  • Teeth stable through the sync — no count or shape shift
  • Ears and earrings present, symmetric, not flickering
  • Hair part on the correct side

Continuity & motion

  • Ring on the same hand as shots 06 / 32
  • Gown strap on the same shoulder throughout
  • Background extras don't walk through geometry
  • Rain direction consistent across the cut
  • Reflections behave — glass, wet asphalt, elevator metal
  • No AI slow-motion drift; movement reads at 24fps

Deliverables

Masters

  • 4K UHD 3840×2160, 16:9, 24fps
  • ProRes 422 HQ, Rec.709 · graded master
  • H.264 40 Mbps · distribution

Cutdowns

  • 9:16 vertical, 2160×3840 — reframe, do not letterbox
  • 1:1 square, 15s and 30s teasers
